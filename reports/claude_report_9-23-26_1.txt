### Findings

**1. Articles of agreement between James Maxwell and his son John Maxwell, 1815 (priority d)**
- Proposed ID: James Maxwell of Toboyne is the **father** of Col. John Maxwell. This agreement settled the 1815 ejectment.
- Record: Cumberland Co. Deeds 1814–1816 (film vols. 1-AA / 1-BB; which volume it is still needs checking), pp. 312–314. DGS 008035090, images 465–466. ARK 3Q9M-CSXB-X9N5-V (image 466: 3Q9M-CSXB-X9JS-N).
- Date / place: 23 May 1815, Toboyne Twp. Entered 7 Nov 1815.
- Verbatim (image 465, p. 313, and image 466, p. 314):
  > Article of Agreement made and agreed to this twenty third day of May AD 1815. Between James Maxwell of Toboine township Cumberland County and State of Pennsylvania of the one part and John Maxwell of the township County and State aforesaid of the other part Whereas the aforesaid James Maxwell is seized and possessed of a certain messuage or tract of land situate in Toboine township aforesaid adjoining lands of Gasper Woolf William Owings and Nicholas Loy and others containing one hundred and eighty four acres and allowance, which the aforesaid James Maxwell doth by these presents bargain and sell the aforesaid tract of land unto the aforesaid John Maxwell his heirs or assigns for the just and full sum of four thousand one hundred and fifty dollars to be paid in the following manner viz. the sum of seven hundred dollars at or upon the first day of July next ensuing this date and and one hundred and fifty at or upon the first day of May one thousand eight hundred and seventeen, and one hundred and fifty dollars at or upon the first day of May one thousand eight hundred and eighteen and one hundred and fifty dollars yearly on the first day of May successively every year until the whole purchase money is all paid the aforesaid James Maxwell his heirs executors or administrators doth obligate themselves to make a good and sufficient deed of Conveyance with a patent out of the land office for the afsd tract of land on the payment of the first hand money and delivers at the same time full and clear possession of said tract of land unto the said John Maxwell, it is also agreed by the said James Maxwell and John his son that under and by the above above Conditions of contract that the afsd James Maxwell has given unto his son John his full share of his estate or the part in full which he the said James ever allows himself to give unto his son John and that in case that he the said James should die intestate that the said John is hereby debared from ever receiving any more of the Estate of the said James Maxwell, it is further agreed between the parties that for the purpose of ending all disputed accounts and reckonings as touching the rents of the fulling mill which has been occupied by the said John Maxwell for a number of years that the said John Maxwell shall pay unto the said James Maxwell or to his heirs the sum of thirty pounds at or upon the first day of May AD 1816, which shall be a final settlement all accts as touching rents … [20 bushels of rye to James; bonds and mortgage for the yearly payments] … bind themselves … in the penal sum of eight thousand three hundred dollars … Witness our hands and seals this twenty third day of May AD 1815 / Witness present — Wm Anderson, Samuel Lin[n], John Milligan / James Maxwell (seal) John Maxwell (seal) / I do Certify that the above is a true Copy of the original article as it remains filed in my office — Wm Anderson / Entered 7 Nov AD 1815. [Recorder's note that he had taken it for an acknowledged original.]
- What it shows: This is the strongest result of the session. John, who rented and ran the fulling mill, was James's **son**, and James gave him his "full share" of the estate. The witnesses are three of the arbitrators in the ejectment (No. 1 below), and the agreement is dated the same day as the arbitration meeting. That makes it the settlement of *James Maxwell v. John Maxwell*.
- Which Maxwell: James Maxwell of Toboyne and Col. John Maxwell. **Confirmed**, since the fulling mill, Toboyne, the ejectment arbitrators, and the 184 acres all match No. 3 below. John had minor children when he died in 1825, so he was born about 1775–90. That puts James's birth about 1745–60, the same generation as Adam (b. 1752). James is therefore very unlikely to be Adam's father, which supports your brother/cousin hypothesis.

**4. Will of Albert Maxwell of Rye Twp, 1788 (a separate James)**
- Record: Cumberland Co. Wills 1750–1803, recorded pp. 172–173 (margin: "old book E p. 144"). Image 377 of 617. ARK 3QS7-L99T-N9KX-G.
- Verbatim:
  > In the name of God Amen I Albert Maxwell of Rye Township Cumberland County and State of Pennsylvania being weak of body but of sound under-standing and memory do this twentyeth day of December one thousand Seven hundred and eighty eight make and publish this my last will and Testament in manner following (viz) First I give devise and bequeath my Plantation I now live upon to my three children that now live with me viz William Maxwell Andrew Maxwell and Margaret Maxwell together with all my personal Estate subject to the payment of all my debts and funeral expenses one half of said plantation I give and devise to my Son William Maxwell & the other half to be equally divided between my said son Andrew and my Daughter Margaret Maxwell and all my personal estate to be equally divided between my three before mentioned children subject to the payments of all my debts and funeral expenses as aforesaid and I do further give devise and bequeath the plantation now in the possession of James Hacket to my five children hereafter named viz James Maxwell Alexander Maxwell Robert Maxwell Ann Maxwell alias Taylor and Mary Maxwell alias Woodburn to be sold at the discretion of my Executors when it may suit the Legatees and to be equally divided among them only five pound apiece to be given to my two sons James Maxwell and Alexander Maxwell over and above the other Legatees to reimburse them for money I had borrowed from them and the crop that is now in the ground in the plantation now occupied by James Hacket I order and allow it to the use of my three children first named (viz) William Maxwell Andrew Maxwell and Margaret Maxwell the better to enable them to pay off my debts and I do constitute and appoint my Trusty friend Frederick Watts Junior and my well beloved sons Alexander Maxwell and William Maxwell Executors … Albert Maxwell (seal) / in the presence of us Robert Walker, Frederick Watts Junior / Be it remembered that on the twenty fourth day of January 1789 the last will and Testament of Albert Maxwell decd was legally proved … letters testamentary issued to Frederick Watts and [William] Maxwell two of the Executors …
- What it shows: Albert had a son **James**, of full age and lending him money by 1788. **No Adam** is named among eight children. The related release, Cumberland Deeds 1796–1800 (ARKs 3Q9M-CSF1-99LF-N and -99LN-M), is dated 25 Jan 1792 and recorded 1797. In it "Alexander Maxwell James Maxwell William Taylor and John Woodburn Legatees of the Estate of Albert Maxwell late deceased of Rye Township" sell to John Pupp. No residences are given.
- Which Maxwell: Albert of Rye is **not** Adam's father (Possible → effectively ruled out). The open question is whether Albert's son James is James of Toboyne. See Questions.

**6. Release by Adam & Elizabeth Maxwell and others, heirs of William Wallace of Fannett Twp, 1791**
- Record: Franklin Co. Deeds, volume filmed as "Deeds 1813" (an abstract gives Deed Book 11 p. 572; not checked against the volume). Image 298 of 338. ARK 3Q9M-CSF1-PSX. Recorded 1815.
- Verbatim:
  > This Indenture made the eleventh day of February in the Year of our Lord one thousand seven hundred and ninety one, By and Between James Wallace of Franklin County Fannet Township of the one part and Catharine Corran Francis and Joanna Elliot, James and Isabella Hill, Adam and Elizabeth Maxwell, William and Anne Cartey, of the other part, Witnesseth that We the said Catharine Corran Francis and Joanna Elliot, James and Isabella Hill, Adam Elizabeth Maxwell and William and Anne Cartey, for and in consideration of the sum of twenty three pounds lawful Money of Pennsylvania to each of us in hand paid the receipt of which we do hereby acknowledge, do for us our heirs and assigns grant bargain and sell alien and confirm to the said James Wallace his heirs and assigns forever, all our right title and interest in and to the real and personal Estate of William Wallace late of Fannet Township deceased …
- The companion record is the will of James Wallace "Esquire" of Fannett Twp: Franklin Will Book [B p. 339 per abstract], image 372, ARK 3QSQ-G991-CQQM. It is dated 1 Apr 1808 and proved 14 Apr 1808. It gives "unto the sons of my Sister Elizabeth Maxwell, alias Wallace, the one sixth of my personal Estate, to be equally divided amongst them". The estate file (ARK 3QSQ-G9SM-N39S, 7 images) holds only the will and appraisal. There are no receipts.
- What it shows: An Adam Maxwell had a wife Elizabeth, née Wallace, daughter of William Wallace of Fannett (Path Valley, just over the mountain from Toboyne). She had sons living in 1808.
- Which Adam: **Possible** that this is your Adam. The wife's name fits, and Elizabeth was living in 1808 and had sons. But nothing places this couple in Westmoreland. If it is him, it ties him to the Fannett/Toboyne area.
